## How to use this

Headings are a starting point, not a claim. Disagree freely.

1. **Wrong category?** Move the line under the right heading.
2. **Same question as another prompt?** Add a shared `#tag` at the end of both lines.
   Invent your own tag names. `#why-us`, `#roommate`, `#overcame-challenge`, `#name-story` — whatever reads naturally.
   Two lines sharing a tag = "an essay answering one could be reused for the other, after school-specific edits".
3. **Unsure?** Add `#unsure`. That is useful data, not a gap.

You do not need to finish. Even one fully-tagged category is enough to test the corroboration gate.
The `auto-tags:` note on each line is what the classifier currently derives — ignore it, or disagree with it.

## What I changed, so you can reverse it

- `→ moved from X` marks a line I moved. X is where the classifier had put it. If you disagree, move it back.
- `#unsure?` marks the 11 calls I was least confident about — those are the ones most worth your attention.
- **Intellectual Curiosity** is populated with prompts asking what fascinates you for its own sake,
  with no major and no school attached. Yale's "topic or idea that excites you" deliberately stayed in
  Why Major, because it is explicitly scoped to "academic areas you selected above".
- **Challenge & Growth** is populated with prompts asking about a difficulty, barrier, setback, or
  change-through-hardship. Note that three prompts carry a `challenge-growth` auto-tag but do **not**
  belong here, and I left them alone: Michigan's "challenge the present", Stanford's "significant
  challenge that society faces" (about the world, not you), and Yale's "opposing view". The auto-tag is
  keyword matching, not comprehension.
- Nothing else changed. Wording, word limits, and schools are untouched.
