# Reuse scoring — authoritative design

**Status: implemented.** This document is the single source of truth for how an
essay is scored against a prompt.

Measured results are in [evaluation/reuse-scoring.md](evaluation/reuse-scoring.md)
and the configuration is chosen by
[evaluation/scoring-sweep.md](evaluation/scoring-sweep.md), regenerated by
`scripts/evaluate-reuse-scoring.mts` and `scripts/sweep-scoring.mts`. The
regression set the design is held to is `src/lib/reuse-cases.ts`.

Everything the original design contributed — a 60-point primary bonus, an
80-point "ready to reuse" threshold, a corroboration gate, a size factor, a
confidence factor, a rank/badge split, and word-count *penalties* — is
**superseded and must not be reimplemented**, and `src/lib/superseded-scoring.test.ts`
greps for their identifiers so they cannot half-survive.

## Why it changed, twice

**The shipped formula was `baseline 20 + familyOverlap 60 + wordCountPenalty`.**
A shared primary category was worth 60 points and `20 + 60 = 80` was exactly
the "ready to reuse" threshold, so two prompts sharing a broad category were
called ready to submit unchanged, with 106 of 255 prompts in one category and
nothing in the score reading the essay.

**Its replacement — four factors at 25 primary / 40 semantic / 20 secondary /
15 function — over-corrected.** Measured on the four Inkstone prompts, which are
one activity-and-service story framed four ways, four of the twelve ordered
pairs came back "New response recommended" and Georgetown against Harvard shared
a primary category and still only reached "Reusable with edits". Three defects,
in descending order of cost:

- **The function factor was all-or-nothing.** `describe` against `reflect`
  forfeited all 15 points for a difference the grouping itself calls "a step,
  not a rewrite".
- **`Other` earned no primary points and switched to a reduced weight vector
  capped at 85**, charging such a pair twice. It was 42% of the effective
  catalogue.
- **Primary ↔ secondary overlap was worth 7 points where a shared primary was
  worth 25** — the right relationship at a quarter of the price, and the reason
  Princeton's service prompt was unreachable from the activity essays that
  answer it.

A fourth defect was in the data rather than the formula: 303 of 553 catalogue
prompts had no classification at all, so 42% resolved to `Other` and 68% had no
secondary theme. Fixing the data alone moved the factors and not one outcome,
which is why both were needed.

## The two scored factors

| Factor | Weight | Scoring | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category compatibility | **35** | graded ladder (below) | `category-review.ts` |
| Semantic similarity | **45** | calibrated z-score (below) | local embedding model |
| Prompt-function fit | **20** | graded; within-group differences nearly free | `category-review.ts` |

`contentFitScore = sum`, range 0–100. **Nothing is subtracted.** Word count,
school-specificity and a cross-group function mismatch are editing cost, not
content mismatch, and act only as band ceilings.

The split is not a preference. `scripts/sweep-scoring.mts` scores eleven
weightings × five function gradings against the 20 calibration positives, the 9
calibration negatives and the aggregate gates; 35/45/20 with within-group
function credit at 0.25 is the only configuration that passes all 29 cases.

### The category ladder

One factor, checked strongest rung first, first match wins. It replaces separate
primary and secondary factors that overlapped — the secondary factor already
paid 7 when one side's primary appeared in the other's secondaries, on top of
whatever primary scored, so the same relationship was priced twice.

| Relationship | Fraction | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Same primary | 1.00 | 35 |
| **One side's primary is the other's stated secondary** | 0.85 | 29.75 |
| Two or more shared secondary families | 0.80 | 28 |
| One shared secondary family | 0.65 | 22.75 |
| Shared tags only | 0.40 + 0.10 each, cap 0.60 | 14–21 |
| No meaningful overlap | 0.15 | 5.25 |
| **A primary nobody recorded, on either side** | 0.50 | 17.5 |

The second rung is what the redesign turns on. The last one is the distinction
that matters most in aggregate:

**A missing category is neutral; `Other` is not.** A missing primary means
nobody looked, which is the absence of a signal and scores the neutral value
semantic and function already use for unknowns. `Other` means a reviewer read
the prompt and found that no category fits — a finding, and one that says this
prompt has no reusable category. Treating them alike handed a free half-weight
to 42% of all pairs and took the share at or above the reuse floor from 10% to
30%. `Other` takes the floor rung and can still be rescued by its secondaries,
which is where a bespoke prompt's reuse signal actually lives.

Neutral cannot rescue a weak match: neutral category plus median semantic
evidence is `new-response`, and reaching the top band on it needs z ≥ 1.5, the
top 5% of the distribution.

**Why Us shares substance only with its own institution.** An institutional-fit
essay's substance *is* the institution, so two Why Us prompts at different
schools share a form and not a word of content. Treated as an ordinary category
it was involved in 27.8% of every top-band pair. Excluding it outright was
wrong, and the demo workspace caught that: an essay written for Brown's Open
Curriculum stopped ranking Brown's own prompt first, because with the category
signal flat across every Why Us prompt nothing distinguished them. Finding the
right prompt for an essay you have written is the same feature as finding a
reuse candidate. So the condition is the institution — read off the
school-specific phrases already detected in the essay's prose — not the
category.

**A hand-maintained related-category table was tried and deleted.** Community ↔
Activities, Activities ↔ Challenge and the rest, at 0.35. Once every prompt had
real secondaries the table stopped earning its place: a prompt that genuinely
overlaps another category now *says so*, which is better evidence than an
assertion about its category, and the sweep prefers it off on both the cases and
the aggregate.

### Format mismatch caps the score at 40

`reading-list`, `roommate` and `shorts` describe a *format* rather than a
subject. When two prompts have different primaries and either is one of those,
**no signal in the formula is evidence of reuse** — not the shared vocabulary
the embeddings see, and not the fact that both prompts happen to say
"describe". Measured: zeroing only the category factor left a five-book list at
50 against a curiosity essay and a roommate note at 65 against a list of five
things, carried by saturated similarity plus an exact function match. The cap is
on the total, and the band is forced to `new-response`.

Two prompts of the *same* format are untouched: a roommate note is a perfectly
good start on another roommate note.

### Factor 2 must be calibrated, never raw cosine

Raw cosine between general-purpose embeddings puts almost any essay/prompt pair
in a narrow band — both are English prose about a person — and comparing a long
narrative to a short question compresses it further. Score each prompt against
that essay's own background distribution instead:

```
z       = (cos(essay, prompt) - mean(cos(essay, allPrompts))) / stdev(...)
factor2 = clamp(0, 45, round(45 * (z + 1) / 3))
```

Assert it: scaling every cosine by a constant must leave all scores unchanged.

Measured across all 251,502 pairs, 1.9% saturate and 12.2% sit at the floor, so
the map is not over-saturating. Harvard against Stanford reaches z = 4.5, the
top 0.05% — and those two prompts are near-paraphrases, so saturating there is
correct. Re-anchoring the `[-1, +2]` window on measured percentiles remains an
open candidate; it was not changed alongside the ladder, because then neither
would be attributable.

**Semantic similarity must not be able to carry a pair alone.** Saturated
similarity plus the category floor has to land below the reuse floor, or an
essay about rebuilding a free library gets recommended for "list five books"
because it is full of the word *books*. That constraint, not taste, is what
holds the semantic weight at 45.

### Factor 2's provider

`@huggingface/transformers` running `Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (q8) locally, in
`src/lib/embedding.ts`. No network at inference, no API key, no essay text
leaving the process. The 553 prompt vectors are precomputed and committed;
essay vectors are computed at save time and cached by content.

**With no provider configured, factor 2 scores neutral and everything still
works.** That is the rollback for the whole stage, and it is the path the test
suite runs on.

Two implementation constraints that are not optional:

- **One text per embedding call.** Batching pads every text to the longest in
  the batch and mean-pools over the padding, so a text's vector depends on its
  batch — cosine 0.991 between the same prompt in two different batches against
  1.000000 embedded twice alone. Committed vectors and runtime essay vectors are
  produced in different passes, so batching would put an error the size of the
  real between-prompt differences into every comparison. Embedding singly is
  also faster.
- **Quantise with a per-vector scale.** A fixed int8 scale maps a typical
  component (~1/√384) onto ~6 of 127 levels, an 8% error measuring cosine 0.988
  against the original. Scaling by each vector's peak costs four characters per
  row and brings it under 0.01%.

### Factor 3 is graded, and both extremes were measured

| Relationship | Points of 20 | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Exact match | 20 | none |
| Difference inside a group | 5 | none |
| Different group | 0 | `reusable-edits` |
| Unknown on either side | 10 (neutral) | none |

Groups are retrospective (`describe`, `reflect`, `explain-impact`,
`demonstrate-growth`) and forward (`explain-motivation`,
`discuss-future-contribution`, `connect-to-school`, `state-a-future-goal`).

A finer per-pair adjacency was attempted and rejected on the evidence: mean
similarity between prompts of different function classes spans only 0.19–0.27,
`describe`/`reflect` falls *below* the median while the cross-group
`discuss-future-contribution`/`reflect` is the highest of all 28 pairs. The
embedding cannot separate what a prompt asks you to *do* from what it is
*about*, so a table derived from it would launder noise. See
[evaluation/factor-analysis.md](evaluation/factor-analysis.md).

Both extremes of the within-group credit fail, which is why it is 0.25:

- At **0** — the original all-or-nothing rule — two of the twenty calibration
  positives fail, because `describe` against `reflect` forfeits the whole
  factor.
- At **1** — function removed from the score and left as a band ceiling only,
  which is what the redesign originally set out to do — function scores full
  marks on 50.2% of all pairs, because there are only two groups. A factor that
  is a near-constant discriminates nothing, and the free points took the share
  at or above the reuse floor from 18% to 32%. It also dropped the calibration
  negatives from 9 of 9 to between 0 and 5, because same-topic-different-ask
  pairs are exactly what function was catching.

"Known" and "inside a group" are both load-bearing. An essay whose function
nobody recorded is neutral, never mismatched, or every essay predating
onboarding is locked out of the top band. And only a cross-group mismatch caps
the band, which is what makes the owner's specification hold: a reflective
Community essay is never "slight edits" for a Community prompt asking what the
student will contribute, however well the topic matches.

## The four bands

"Ready to reuse" does not exist. Essentially every reused essay needs some
tailoring, so no label claims otherwise.

| Score | Internal state | Student-facing label |
|---|---|---|
| 70–100 | `reusable-slight-edits` | Reusable with slight edits |
| 60–69 | `reusable-edits` | Reusable with edits |
| 50–59 | `reusable-significant-edits` | Reusable with significant edits |
| < 50 | `new-response` | New response recommended |

**The 60–69 / 50–59 split is load-bearing and must not be collapsed.** 68 means
"a reasonably strong foundation"; 52 means "substantial material is salvageable
but expect major rewriting". Those are different pieces of advice.

## Band ceilings

Three conditions cap the band irrespective of score. They compose:

```
band = min(bandFromScore, ...ceilings)
```

| Condition | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| School-specificity risk **high** | `reusable-significant-edits` |
| School-specificity risk **medium** | `reusable-edits` |
| Known **cross-group** function mismatch | `reusable-edits` |
| Format mismatch (one side a list or a note) | `new-response`, and the score capped at 40 |
| Word count: retention < 0.15 | `reusable-significant-edits` |
| Word count: retention 0.15–0.35 | `reusable-edits` |
| More than 25% below a stated minimum | `reusable-edits` |
| No stated minimum, fill < 0.30 | `reusable-edits` |
| No stated minimum, fill < 0.15 | `new-response` |

### Word count is editing cost

`retention = promptMaxWords / essayWordCount`. Cutting and tightening are
ordinary reuse work, so a 500-word essay against a 300-word prompt (retention
0.60) carries **no ceiling at all** and can reach the top band on merit. Only
fundamental compression caps it.

| Example | Retention | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 500 → 400 | 0.80 | none |
| 500 → 300 | 0.60 | none |
| 500 → 250 | 0.50 | none |
| 500 → 200 | 0.40 | none |
| 500 → 150 | 0.30 | `reusable-edits` |
| 500 → 50 | 0.10 | `reusable-significant-edits` |

Boundaries settled at 0.35 and 0.15 after measurement. The owner marked 500→250
"likely" unrestricted and 500→150 "potentially" capped, and an earlier 0.45/0.60
pair fired on most of the demo workspace - capping more pairs than the score was
deciding, which means the score had stopped mattering. The principle is what is
fixed: *reasonable shortening is normal editing; fundamental compression is
substantial rewriting.*

**Under-length stays more restrictive**, because you cannot condense your way up
to a length you have not written. But "more restrictive" is not "on a hair
trigger": schools state a maximum rather than a target, so a 300-word essay
against a 650-word maximum (fill 0.46) is a legitimate answer and carries no
ceiling, and a 590-word essay against a 600-word minimum is a paragraph short
rather than an adaptation cost.

The `fill < 0.15 → new-response` row preserves a defect the codebase already
fixed once: a 15-word note scored 80 against a 650-word prompt and was
recommended as ready to reuse. Such an essay is not shortened, it is not yet
written. **Removing the word-count penalty without this ceiling reintroduces
that bug**, and no later relaxation may remove it.

### Function mismatch

A known **major** function mismatch prevents the top band. The specification is
the owner's example: a reflective Community essay must not be labelled *Reusable
with slight edits* for a Community prompt asking what the student will contribute
in future, however strongly topic and themes overlap.

Needed because `25 + 35 + 20 = 80` reaches the top band with no function fit.

Two words are doing work:

- **"Known"** — an essay whose function is unknown is *neutral, never
  mismatched*. No cap, no penalty. Otherwise every essay predating onboarding is
  permanently locked out of the top band.
- **"Major"** — `describe → reflect` is a small step; `reflect → discuss-future-
  contribution` is a large one. Build a **mismatch-severity matrix** over the
  eight functions in `category-review.ts`; only major distance caps the band,
  minor distance merely forfeits the 20 points. **Fallback if the matrix cannot
  be justified by evaluation: any mismatch caps.**

An essay's function comes from `origin_prompt_id` (the nullable FK in the
onboarding work). If a student says which prompt an essay was written for, its
function is known exactly.

## Where the essay's own signal comes from

`src/lib/essays.ts` hardcodes `source: "manual"` for every essay family link and
no code path auto-classifies an essay. So essay-side categories are whatever the
student chose, and **"reset to the automatic suggestion" cannot work for essays
until `classifyText` is wired into essay create/update.** That wiring is part of
this work, not a pre-existing capability.

## Evaluation — gates implementation

### Part 1: structural (65,025-pair catalogue cross-product)

Retained, and **explicitly not sufficient**: both sides are catalogue prompts, so
it proves the formula is well-behaved and nothing about whether the advice is
useful. Report:

1. Percentage of pairs in each of the four bands.
2. Band distribution for `Other` specifically, against the non-`Other` baseline.
3. Examples of `Other` pairs reaching 70+, with factor breakdowns — if they do
   not read as genuinely reusable, the `Other` vector is too generous.
4. Boundary examples at 50, 60 and 70, several either side. A 61 and a 59 must be
   visibly different situations or the band split is not real.
5. **False-positive top-band matches** — pairs scoring 70+ a reader would judge
   unreusable. The primary failure mode; hunt it deliberately.
6. Known function-mismatch cases, and confirmation that unknown never caps.
7. Ordinary vs extreme word-count change, and where the retention boundary
   actually falls.
8. Whether strong normal-category matches remain highly ranked.
9. How many pairs cross the raised `new-response` floor (30 → 50).
10. Ceiling attribution: how many pairs are limited by each ceiling, and whether
    any ceiling is doing so much work the score barely matters.

### Part 2: product validation

- **Real essay → prompt matching**, judged by reading the output.
- **Portfolio coverage:** can a diverse portfolio of ~6 essays provide legitimate
  reusable material for a substantial majority of a student's supplemental
  prompts? Run per-school on a realistic 8–12 college list. This is the question
  the product exists to answer, and no aggregate statistic reveals it. A formula
  that scores beautifully and leaves a six-essay portfolio covering 20% of
  prompts has failed.

**Neither part can be replaced by unit tests passing.**

## Acceptance criteria

1. A shared primary alone scores 35 → `new-response`. It reaches the top band
   only with strong semantic evidence *and* a function that fits.
2. A perfect score is exactly 100, and there is exactly one weight vector, so no
   band boundary can shift silently.
3. A cross-group function mismatch keeps the reflective-Community vs
   future-contribution-Community pair out of the top band, asserted by the
   ceiling independently of the score.
4. A missing primary scores the neutral rung; `Other` scores the floor rung.
   Those are different situations and the code must not conflate them.
5. Two Why Us prompts at different schools score the floor rung; a Why Us essay
   still ranks its own school's prompt first.
6. **No score decreases from word count.** `contentFitScore` is identical for a
   500-word essay against 300-word and 500-word prompts of the same content;
   only the ceiling and the adaptation label differ.
7. A 15-word essay against a 650-word no-minimum prompt resolves to
   `new-response`.
8. A prompt whose limit is stated in pages or sentences — 41 of them, with null
   numeric columns — reads as `minimal` adaptation, not as needing shortening.
9. With no provider configured, every pair still scores and ranks.
10. Committed prompt vectors are byte-stable; a model change is a reviewed
    commit. Scaling every cosine by a constant leaves all scores unchanged.
11. No `ready-to-reuse`, `minor-adaptation`, `major-adaptation`, `sizeFactor` or
    `confidenceFactor` identifier survives in `src/` — a grep assertion, so the
    superseded design cannot half-survive.
12. All 20 calibration positives and all 9 calibration negatives pass, negatives
    on score *and* band; top-band share stays at or below 6.5% and the share at
    or above the reuse floor at or below 20%.

## What is still outstanding

Every number here uses catalogue prompts as stand-ins for essays. **Real essays
scored and read by a person is the one check that catches a formula which is
internally consistent and practically useless, and it has not been done.** A
formula that scores beautifully and leaves a six-essay portfolio covering 20% of
prompts has failed, and no aggregate statistic reveals that.

The embedding model is the next lever with the largest expected gain and is
deliberately unchanged here, so that this redesign is attributable on its own.
